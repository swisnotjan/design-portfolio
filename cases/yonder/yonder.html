Yonder — world clocks that live in your menu bar

A native macOS app for tracking world clocks and teammates' timezones. I designed it and shipped it myself — in code, with AI-assisted development — and distributed it via Homebrew with its own product site.

A solo project — design, frontend and release, end to end.

Why

Working across the UK and US, “what time is it for them right now?” was a question I asked a dozen times a day. Every tool I tried was either a heavy calendar add-on or a web page I forgot to open. I wanted something that lived where I already look — the menu bar — and disappeared the rest of the time.

[ media — Yonder in the macOS menu bar ]

The shape of it

One glanceable list: people and cities, their local time, and whether it's a reasonable hour to ping them. A quiet color cue does the social math — green for “go ahead”, dim for “they're probably asleep”. No accounts, no setup wizard, no notifications begging for attention.

[ media — the dropdown: people, times, day/night cue ]
Designing in code changed the work: I could feel latency, hit-targets and animation timing directly, and tune them in the same loop as the visuals — no handoff gap.

Designing in code

I built Yonder in SwiftUI with AI-assisted development — using AI tools to move from intent to working interface quickly, then refining by hand. The result is a real, native app: system-accurate type, motion and behavior, not a mockup pretending to be one.

“Shipping it myself meant the craft survived all the way to the build.”

Getting it out there

I packaged it for Homebrew so the people most likely to want it — developers and designers on distributed teams — could install it with one line, and gave it a small dedicated site to explain itself in a single scroll.
